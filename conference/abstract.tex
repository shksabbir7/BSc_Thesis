\begin{abstract}
Authentic audience commentary is unavailable before viewers encounter a film, while fluent model output alone establishes neither controllability nor predictive validity. We present a bounded neuro-symbolic multi-agent framework that generates short Bangla cinema responses at one of two requested engagement-specificity levels. Corpus analysis rejected a persona interpretation, and length-matched human judgments supported the resulting distinction. The workflow retrieves examples from R1 and uses a frozen LaBSE probe, Verifier-A, for bounded revision. A BanglaBERT outcome model, Verifier-B, is trained on disjoint R2 data and never enters generation. The experiment produced 5,400 outputs from 90 held-out plots, two levels, ten conditions, and three paired seeds. All nine registered alternatives improved Verifier-B target probability over zero-shot after multiple-comparison correction. Neural gating with symbolic feedback produced the largest contrast, $+0.2570$ (95\% paired-bootstrap CI $[0.2151,0.2987]$), although its direct advantage over neural-only gating remains exploratory. During 147 same-case revisions, the Verifier-A--Verifier-B gap widened by 0.1828. Three blinded native-Bangla annotators recovered the requested level on a balanced 100-item subset with 0.9133 accuracy (95\% CI $[0.8667,0.9567]$). The results support control of a human-recognizable textual distinction, not prediction of audience reception.
\end{abstract}
